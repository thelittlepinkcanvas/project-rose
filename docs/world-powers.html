World

Powers

Origin

300 years ago, a shower of small meteorites fell across the world, scattering fragments over countless regions. These meteor fragments emitted a unique form of radiation that interacted with human DNA, triggering genetic mutations in a portion of the population. As a result, some individuals awakened extraordinary abilities.

In the beginning, power users were feared as monsters. Many were captured and subjected to experiments as scientists tried to understand the source of their abilities. Over time, power users united and resisted their oppression. After years of conflict, they earned recognition and secured the right to live alongside ordinary humans.

As generations passed, the number of power users steadily increased, while the population of non-power users gradually declined. In the present day, power users make up the majority of society and are considered the norm. They hold most positions of influence in politics, economic, and social institutions, while those without power have become a minority and are sidelined.


Inheritance

Powers are inherited genetically. If both parents are power users, the child would always be a power user. If either one of the parents is a power user, the child would have a 50% chance of being a power user. If neither parent is a power user, the child will not develop powers.


Awakening

Most power users awaken their abilities between the ages of five and seven. However, there have been rare documented cases in which powers emerge during adolescence or even adulthood. Individuals who awaken their abilities later than usual are known as Late Bloomers.


Uniqueness

Every power is unique.

Since the appearance of the first power users three centuries ago, there has never been a confirmed case of two individuals possessing the same ability. While some powers may appear similar in function, they always differ in their mechanics, limitations, applications, or side effects.


Side Effects

Although powers are useful, they come with side effects that differ from each person. The side effects range from mild inconveniences to severe physical or psychological consequences. Some abilities may only cause temporary fatigue after use, while others place significant strain on the user's body or mind.

According to current research, physical side effects are by far the most common. Mental side effects, though considerably rarer, are often more unpredictable and can be especially dangerous.


Current Research

Scientists and researchers around the world continue to investigate the genetic differences between power users and non-power users, as well as the biological mechanisms that allow powers to exist. Despite centuries of research, many fundamental questions about the exact process by which powers are formed still remain unanswered.


Law

Under Foraville law, every power user is required to register their ability with the Ministry of Powers.

The registration system records basic information about each individual's ability, allowing the government to maintain an official database of registered power users. Failure to register is considered a criminal offence and may result in fines or imprisonment.

To assist with criminal investigations, high-ranking police individuals are granted access to the registry.
