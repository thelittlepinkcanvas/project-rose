Country Background

The Origin

A century ago, the world was filled with war. A group of ships from a fallen country, carrying its remaining citizens, reached the shore of this inhabited land. The leaders of each ship gathered and decided to let the people stay permanently in this land. They named the land Foraville, inspired by its rich abundance of flowers.


The first generation of Floras

The settlers came to call themselves Floras, a name proposed by Aster Sunfloria, one of the five founding leaders.

The first generation was led by 5 leaders: Aster Sunfloria, Earl Lavinder, Milton Periwinkle, Dahlia Lotus and Elowynn Bluebell.

Each leader governed a different region of Foraville, and each district was named in honour of its respective founder. The entire first generation consisted of only eighty settlers.


Economy

Foraville's economy is built upon its abundant natural resources. Its primary exports include agricultural products, minerals, and medicinal plants. All are traded with neighbouring countries through international agreements.


Currency

The official currency of Foraville is the Petal, used in the form of paper notes. Smaller denominations are issued as coins known as bud.


Political system

Since the nation's founding, leadership of each region has been passed down through the bloodline of its respective founding family.

The position of Head of Foraville, the nation's highest authority, is selected every five years from among the five regional leaders. Rather than through election, the position rotates in a fixed cycle between the five ruling families.

This system was established during the second generation after a dispute arose over who should hold the nation's highest authority. The conflict escalated into a civil war that lasted for three years. To prevent future power struggles, the rotational leadership system was introduced and has remained in place ever since.


The national problem

One of Foraville's longest-standing social issues is the discrimination between power users and non-power users.

During the first generation, this divide was nonexistent. Power users and non-power users worked side by side to build the nation. The founding leaders believed that every citizen deserved equal respect regardless of whether they possessed power.

Unfortunately, these ideals were not inherited by the next generation.

As time passed, discrimination grew increasingly severe. Non-power users were treated as inferior citizens, stripped of many of their rights, and often forced into lives of hardship and fear. In the worst periods of history, many were regarded as servants.

The situation did not begin to improve until the fifth generation of leaders, who introduced reforms requiring non-power users to be treated with dignity and protected under the law.

Although these reforms ended the most extreme forms of oppression, prejudice remained deeply rooted within society. Even today, despite legal equality, many non-power users continue to face discrimination, making it one of Foraville's most persistent social challenges.
